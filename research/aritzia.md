Quick math (assumption, our calculation): at FY26 revenue, **every 1 point of gross margin ≈ $37M**. Markdowns are one of the drivers management names.

## Aritzia has already paid for slow demand response

In 2023, inventory arrived "earlier and heavier than planned" and was concentrated in client favourites rather than new styles. Aritzia cleared it with markdowns, FY24 gross margin fell to **38.5%**, and one quarter grew just 1.7% [S18]. An analyst's comment, reported in [S18]: you can't have "accessible luxury" alongside more markdowns. *Secondary source; confirm in Aritzia's FY24 annual report.*

Since then, margin has recovered to 44.9%, and management credits **inventory management** and **lower markdowns** [S17]. Getting stock to match demand is clearly a priority they talk about publicly.

## Why the store floor matters at Aritzia

- **Clienteling model:** Style Advisors serve clients one on one and pull items from stock for them. Every request ("this in a 4, in black") is a precise statement of demand, **made before any sale happens**.
- **65% of revenue is in boutiques** [S17], so most demand is expressed in a store, not online.
- **Omnichannel depends on store stock being accurate.** Aritzia offers buy-online-pick-up-in-store and ship-from-store at most boutiques [S19]. Our interviews found items "stuck" in returns processing and orders that "never come".
- **New stores have no history to forecast from.** About 13 new boutiques a year, mostly in new US markets [S17]. Forecasts built on past sales are weakest exactly there. *(Our inference.)*

## What we heard on the floor

See the [interview note](interviews/2026-10-19-aritzia-store-team.md):
- Retrieve requests go through CMT, which has **no notifications**. Staff refresh by hand.
- **At peak, staff skip CMT and ask out loud**, so requests go unrecorded when demand is highest.
- Tools are unreliable (3/10 and 5/10 ratings).

## The opportunity (hypothesis)

**Sales data only shows demand that was met.** When a client asks for a size that isn't there, no transaction happens, so forecasting and allocation never see it. Aritzia's tools already capture these requests in CMT, but staff skip the tool at peak and the data stays inside the store. Head office has no view of this lost demand.

So the gap between **demand shifting** (clients asking for a style the store can't supply) and **the business responding** (allocation moving stock or reordering) is long. The first signal usually doesn't come until weekly sell-through reports show the style has sold out.

## Framing note for the pitch

The case asks what **SAP** should build for **retailers**. Use Aritzia as the persona and design partner (real workflow, real quotes, public financials), but pitch a product for SAP's fashion customers generally. Avoid naming Aritzia's internal tool failures in the public submission; describe them as "store clienteling tools" and keep interviewees anonymous.
