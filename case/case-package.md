# Case package: Sensing the Shift

Transcribed from [Product+ 2026 Case Package.pdf](Product+%202026%20Case%20Package.pdf) (5 pages). Case window: **Oct 18 – Oct 25, 2026**.

> **Sensing the Shift.** Bridging the gap between real-time data and operational action in the era of dynamic retail.

## Challenge statement

> **How might SAP help retailers close the gap between when demand shifts and when the business responds?**

Teams are encouraged to interpret this broadly. The problem space covers inventory management, supply chain coordination, in-store operations, customer experience and more. Strong solutions:

- identify a **specific user and problem**
- develop a **well-reasoned product concept**
- articulate the **value for both the retailer and their customers**

### Things to think about (from the case)

| Theme | Question the case asks |
|---|---|
| Retail context and business value | What specific challenges do retailers face, and how would your solution create **measurable** value for the business and its customers? |
| SAP's role and capabilities | How could your solution fit within **SAP's broader enterprise ecosystem** and help retailers turn data into actionable decisions? |
| AI, automation and risk | Where could AI or automation improve decision-making, and **where is human oversight still important**? What risks or trade-offs should be considered? |

## About SAP (as given in the case)

- World's leading provider of enterprise application software: finance, supply chain, procurement, HR, customer experience.
- **440,000+ customers** in **180 countries**.
- **AI strategy (2026):** Joule, SAP's AI copilot embedded across the product suite.
- **Vision: the Autonomous Enterprise.** Moving enterprise software from a system that *records what happened* to one that helps businesses **sense, decide and act in real time**.

## Problem space (as given in the case)

**Market dynamics and the intelligence gap**
- Retail is where the gap between data and decision-making is most costly.
- Consumer demand shifts faster than ever, supply chains remain volatile, margins are thin.
- Retailers collect huge amounts of data (stores, e-commerce, suppliers, logistics), but turning it into **timely, confident action** is the core challenge.
- AI is moving from experimentation into operations. The question is *how* to integrate it so it improves how teams work, earns customer trust and stays resilient when conditions change unexpectedly.

**Human decision-making under pressure.** The users the case names:
- **Merchandising managers** balancing inventory across channels
- **Supply chain planners** responding to disruptions
- **Store teams** trying to deliver consistent experiences under pressure

> "Decision-makers with real accountability, working with imperfect information and limited time."
